## Judge check (12 Nov 2026): a second judge on a stratified sample

The Lab's assigned audit drew 64 judged diagnoses (12 per R3 configuration, 4 from the earlier `R3 B2 +ROUTING`
label, 12 pooled from round 2; half judge-pass, half judge-fail per stratum) and re-judged them with `openai/gpt-5.4`
through SREGym's own judge (same reference answer, only the judge model changes).

- Agreement 56 of 64 = 88% (Wilson 77% to 94%), Cohen's kappa 0.75. Original pass rate on the sample 31/64, GPT-5.4 23/64.
- All 8 disagreements are original pass, GPT-5.4 fail: storage_user_unregistered-2 (4), psa_restricted_blocks_recreation (3),
  expired_tls (1). Round 2 pooled: 12 of 12 agree.
- By stratum: F 11/12, R(+FLAT) 9/12, P 10/12, PR 11/12, old R 3/4.
- Reading: our judge is the more lenient one, so reported diagnosis rates may be optimistic. Which judge is right on the
  8 is not settled; a person reading those 8 against the cards would settle it. The sample is too small to say whether
  the leniency favours one configuration.

119 of the 132 lenient disagreements are in four problems: psa_restricted 45, storage_user_unregistered-2 42,
service_port_conflict 19, liveness_probe 13.

What this does and does not show. It shows the two judges differ in one direction, and that the direction matters for
the only gain step 2 had (procedures over routing on diagnosis, 9:2 to 5:3). It does not show which judge is right:
both are LLMs. SREGym's paper validated its judge prompt against an expert on 100 diagnoses (kappa 0.90 with its
default Claude Sonnet-4.6, 0.70 with GPT-5.4, 0.76 with Kimi K2.5; arXiv:2605.07161, section 2.5 and Table 2); our
first judge, `gemini-3.5-flash-lite`, has no such validation. The 40-item hand-label check
(`experiments/judge-audit/`) is the step that can say which judge a person agrees with on the disputed diagnoses.

## Hand labels on 40 step-2 diagnoses (14 Nov 2026)

The first author labeled the 40-item sheet (procedure and limits: `experiments/judge-audit/README.md`), blind to both judges,
answering "right place" and "right cause" per diagnosis; correct means both. Labels as submitted, before any discussion:
`experiments/judge-audit/judge-check-40-labels-first-author.json`. 21 correct, 19 incorrect, 14 marked "not sure".

| Items | Hand label agrees with the first judge | with GPT-5.4 |
|---|---|---|
| 13 where the judges agree (7 pass, 6 fail) | 13 | 13 |
| 27 where they disagree | 13 | 14 |

By fault, on the 27 disputed (first judge : GPT-5.4): psa_restricted 0:9, storage_user_unregistered-2 4:4,
service_port_conflict 3:0, liveness_probe 2:1, feature_flag 2:0, expired_tls 2:0.

Reading: on these items a person does not side with one judge. GPT-5.4 is stricter about where the fault is placed
(psa_restricted: the text blames the container's settings, the ground truth is the namespace label); the first judge
accepts a diagnosis that names the right object and mechanism without every detail (expired_tls, feature_flag). So
"the first judge is wrong 132 times" is not supported; "the two judges apply the rubric with different strictness, and
a result that depends on which one is used is not robust" is.

Sensitivity, not used in the abstract. A second reading by the AI assistant, which was not blind (it knew which faults
the judges dispute), questions seven of the "correct" labels: four storage items (H08, H25, H34, H39) say the `rate`
service lacks credentials, the same claim as the four storage items marked incorrect; three port-conflict items
(H19, H20, H37) say an old `media-service` pod holds port 9100, while the ground truth is the node-exporter. With the
four storage labels changed the split is 9:18; with all seven, 6:21. The abstract reports the labels as submitted
(13:14), because changing labels after a non-blind review would bias the count toward the paper's own claim. A second
blind labeler is the proper fix and is written into the frozen 30 plan (`docs/plan/19`).

### A third reading, blind (14 Nov 2026)

To check the labels without the assistant's knowledge of the results, the first author gave the same 40 diagnoses and the ground truth
per fault (`experiments/judge-audit/blind-second-reading-40.md`: no judge names, no verdicts, none of the labels) to a
separate AI session with no project context. It is an AI reading, not a human label, and it is not used in the abstract.
Verdicts: `experiments/judge-audit/judge-check-40-third-reading-blind-ai.json` (18 correct, 22 incorrect).

| Items | Third reading agrees with the first judge | with GPT-5.4 |
|---|---|---|
| 13 where the judges agree | 13 | 13 |
| 27 where they disagree | 10 | 17 |

It agrees with the hand label on 27 of 40. The 13 differences fall in three groups:

- It fails seven the hand label passed: the four storage items (H08, H25, H34, H39: the text blames the `rate` service's
  credentials) and three port-conflict items (H19, H20, H37: the text says an old `media-service` pod holds the port).
  These are the same seven the non-blind reading questioned.
- It fails H38 (feature flag named, but the failure is placed in the call to the search backend). The hand label stays "correct":
  the text names the flag in the frontend's ConfigMap as the cause, and the fix it implies is the right one.
- It passes five psa_restricted items the hand label failed (H10, H13, H14, H22, H24), drawing the line at whether the text says the
  namespace enforces the policy. The hand label required more: all of them present the deployment's settings as what is wrong,
  while the ground truth says the deployment is healthy and the namespace label is the fault.

Three readings, same picture: on the disputed items nobody sides with one judge throughout (hand label 13:14,
blind AI 10:17, non-blind AI reading of the hand labels 6:21). Where the readings differ, the cause is how strictly "right place" is
applied, which is also what separates the two judges. On the 14 disputed items where the hand label and the blind reading
agree, 9 match GPT-5.4 and 5 the first judge.
